# Outreach Intel

## Purpose

Outreach fails in two boring ways: drafts that quietly go stale, and contact lists built on guesses. This skill fixes both. It triages what you already started, and it builds contact lists only from relationships you can verify.

## Part 1: Triage the draft pileup

1. List every draft across your connected email accounts. For each one note the recipient, the subject, a one-line purpose, and its approximate age.
2. Check thread liveness in sent mail before judging any draft. Look for replies, resolutions, or expired context, such as a passed event or a closed deal.
3. Sort each draft into exactly one bucket:
   - **Still live**: needs action or sending.
   - **Dead**: resolved, passed, or obsolete, including accidental reply drafts on newsletters and no-reply addresses.
   - **Needs nudge**: waiting on someone else, or on one input from you.
   - **Needs review**: ambiguous, needs your eyes before anyone decides.
4. Some drafts are not drafts. A saved credential note, a medication list, or a bookmark kept as a draft gets flagged as **Keep as note**. Never reveal its contents, and never touch it.
5. Deletion is always opt-in, never automatic. If you offer it, snapshot anything with real content to a note first, and say plainly what will be deleted and why before deleting anything.

## Part 2: Mine verified contacts

1. Search your email for threads with brands, agencies, PR firms, and past sponsors across your beats, for example music, culture, tech, lifestyle, and beauty. Extract the name, title, company, email, and the nature of the history.
2. Search your shared drive for outreach sheets, decks, and contact lists. Note each sheet's date. Titles and roles go stale, so re-verify old entries before using them.
3. Rank warm first. A real reply thread from last year beats a famous name with no history, every time.
4. Deliver one ranked list with these columns: name, title, company, email or profile link, warmth, source, and notes.

## Verification rules (the whole point)

1. Never invent a name, title, email address, or relationship. If it cannot be verified in your email or drive, it does not go on the list.
2. Flag stale data for re-verification instead of silently including it.
3. Keep one running warmth log per outreach lane, so the next mining pass starts from verified history instead of from zero.
4. When a triage pass surfaces a live sponsor or partner thread, roll its contacts straight into the ranked list instead of treating the two jobs as separate.

## Sending rules

1. Draft pitches only. This skill never sends email on your behalf. The human does the final review and sends.
2. Report with the bottom line first: money and time-sensitive items go at the top.
3. The human decides every action. The list is intelligence, not instructions.

## Example

A pre-sponsorship pass over one inbox and drive produces:

| 1 | Alex Example | Example Records | Replied last spring | Email thread | Asked for rates, never received them. Follow up first. |
| 2 | Jordan Sample | Sample Agency | Past sponsor | Drive contact sheet, dated last year | Title needs re-verification before outreach. |

And a draft triage summary that leads with the bottom line: 34 drafts found, 6 still live, 2 need a nudge this week, 19 dead, 4 kept as notes, 3 need review.
