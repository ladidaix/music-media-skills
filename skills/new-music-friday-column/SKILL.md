# New Music Friday Column

## Purpose

Produce a weekly New Music Friday (NMF) column in a repeatable format, built in an editable document with publication-ready hyperlinks, so the column can go straight from draft to publish with one edit pass.

## Workflow

1. Start from your own recent archive. Match the established format, section order, and linking pattern of your last few editions before changing anything.
2. Gather the week's releases and your takes on each one. A take can be two sharp sentences. It cannot be invented.
3. Build the piece in an editable document, never only as chat copy, with hyperlinks placed as you write.
4. Split the work across two days (see the timing rule below): copy first, link pass second.
5. Build the cover last, once the featured artists are locked.

## Format rules

### Title

Use one consistent title format every week:

`New Music Friday (Month D): Artist, Artist, Artist, and More`

1. Every title carries the words "New Music Friday" and the release date.
2. The artists named in the title must span genres, for example pop, hip-hop, rock, and R&B in a single title. Never stack the title with one genre.
3. For a multi-week catch-up edition, prefix the standard title with a catch-up hook and a double colon, for example: `12 Releases You Missed:: New Music Friday (Month D to Month D): Artist, Artist, and More`

### Entries

1. Each featured release gets a clean, bold header with the artist and release name. Headers carry no hyperlinks.
2. The body paragraph under each header is where the links, the context, and your take live.
3. End the column with a short "also out" line for releases that deserve a mention but not a full entry. Those artists get links too.

## Hyperlink rules

1. A hyperlink goes on the first mention of the artist's name in the body paragraph, never on the entry header line.
2. Every artist covered gets a link, featured entries and the "also out" line alike.
3. Each link must point to the specific release being covered, a direct album or release page wherever one exists. A search results page is only a fallback while a direct page genuinely cannot be created yet, never the default.
4. Before relying on an embedded player or card in your publishing platform, check that the source domain is on the platform's approved embed list. If it is not, use a plain text hyperlink and do not retest the embed every week.

## The timing rule (release-week link lag)

Most Friday releases do not appear in streaming and music catalogs until roughly midnight to 3 a.m. on release day, so their catalog pages cannot exist before then. Plan for it instead of fighting it:

1. Thursday: do the full copy edit pass. Leave the link slots alone.
2. Friday morning: search each release inside the catalog (the search itself often creates the page), confirm the page is live, and paste the links in.

## Cover art

The cover is a scrapbook-style collage, not a grid:

1. Use full person cut-outs with raw, curved edges, layered and overlapping at varied scales over a torn-paper or parchment background.
2. No grid, no white lines or gutters, and no blocky tiles. A plain text title card is too plain.
3. Use photos only from the featured artists' own public social accounts, or from photos you have the rights to use. Never use press, editorial, or agency photos you do not have a license for.
4. Put the words "New Music Friday" and the date line on the cover itself, in a bold display typeface.
5. Credit the photo sources in your platform's photo credit field, in a format like `NMF (@handle1, @handle2 via Instagram)`. Keep credits out of the caption text.

## Voice and quality bar

1. Use contractions, the Oxford comma, and no em dashes.
2. Never invent quotes, facts, release details, or biographical claims. If a detail cannot be verified against a primary source, leave it out.
3. Verify every release title, artist name spelling, and release date against the release itself before drafting.
4. Match the archived format before innovating. Consistency is what makes a weekly column feel like a column.

## Output contract

Deliver:

1. An editable document with the full column, hyperlinks in place, formatted to the pattern above.
2. The finished cover image.
3. A short done and waiting-on note: what is ready to publish, and what still needs the editor's takes or approval.

## Example

Title: `New Music Friday (June 6): Maya Rivers, Kade, The Hollow Pines, and More`

One entry, with the link on the first body mention and a clean header:

**Maya Rivers, "Second Summer"**

[Maya Rivers](https://example.com/maya-rivers-second-summer) recorded this one in a garage studio in July, and you can hear the heat in it. Twelve tracks, no skips, and a closer that sounds like driving home with the windows down.
