# Interview Production

## Purpose

Turn a raw interview recording into a package an editor can cut from immediately: clean transcripts, captions, and a short, decisive list of the moments worth publishing.

## Step 1: Transcribe the recording

1. Pull the audio or video from the shared drive folder or upload the host provides.
2. For video, strip the audio track first, then transcribe the audio.
3. For long recordings, ask which parts matter most, transcribe those first, and then do the rest.
4. Produce three files:
   - a `.docx` transcript for editors and producers,
   - a `.md` transcript for search, quoting, and reuse, and
   - `.srt` captions with medium-length cues of about 5 seconds each, cut at sentence boundaries, with every cue ending on a complete thought.
5. Flag anything that was off the record, and mark it clearly in the transcript instead of quietly including or deleting it.

## Step 2: Pick the edit selects

1. Read the full transcript for the most engaging moments: the strongest story, the sharpest opinion, the line people will quote.
2. Propose a cold open of about 15 seconds, built by stitching 2 to 4 moments together. Present the stitch order as a numbered list with timestamps.
3. Propose social clips of 15 to 60 seconds each. For every clip give:
   - the timestamp range,
   - a search-friendly title written the way a viewer would search for it, and
   - keyword tags.
4. Confirm the picks with the host or editor before finalizing. They narrow the list. Keep their reordered cold open as the standing example for that guest instead of re-proposing from scratch next time.
5. Write the final selects as notes at the top of the transcript files, and park the runners-up below the final picks so nothing good is lost.

## Step 3: Deliver the package

Organize everything in one folder named `<Guest> Interview`, or `<Guest> Interview Clips` for a social-only set. The package contains:

1. The docx and markdown transcripts, with the clip notes at the top.
2. The SRT captions.
3. A clip notes list: the cold-open order, plus the final social clips with timestamps, titles, and tags.
4. A short done and waiting-on note: what is ready for the video editor, and what the host still needs to decide.

## Operating rules

1. Selects and files only. This skill does not render video clips. The video editor does the edit. Your job is to make their edit fast and obvious.
2. Apply clean house style to production notes and clip titles. If the package includes the writer’s own written copy, flag grammar and mechanical issues and offer take-or-leave stronger-writing suggestions; the writer decides, and their text is never rewritten. Transcripts stay faithful to what was actually said.
3. Never invent quotes, facts, or biographical details. A clip title may be punchy, but it may not promise something the guest did not say.
4. Every package ships accessible: full transcripts, captions, and alt text for any thumbnail or cover image.
5. If the social cuts are headed for a quote carousel, hand the selects to a carousel workflow instead of formatting slides inside this one.

## Example

A 42-minute interview with an indie artist produces:

1. `Artist Interview.docx` and `Artist Interview.md`, with the clip notes on page one.
2. `Artist Interview.srt`, cues at sentence boundaries, about 5 seconds each.
3. A cold open: 0:41 to 0:47 (the one-line origin story), 18:02 to 18:06 (the laugh), 31:15 to 31:20 (the thesis of the new album).
4. Three social clips, each with a timestamp range, a searchable title such as “How [Artist] Wrote the Album in a Garage Studio,” and tags.
5. A done and waiting-on note: transcripts and captions ready for the editor, cold-open order waiting on the host’s final pick between two stitches.
