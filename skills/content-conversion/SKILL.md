# Content Conversion

## Purpose

A piece of substance gets created once, by the human. This skill does everything after that: the conversion, the selection, and the per-platform optimization. It works in two lanes, written work to video, and long-form video to social clips. It never creates substance. Every word in a converted package traces back to the human’s written piece or recorded words.

## The boundary (read first, it governs everything below)

1. Selection, conversion, and optimization only. No generated substance, ever.
2. This skill never writes a script, an intro line, a transition, a hook, or voiceover text. If a package needs a word, the word comes from the source, or the gap is flagged for the human to fill in their own words.
3. Verbatim means verbatim. Pulled lines keep the writer’s exact wording. If a line looks too long or a caption has a grammar slip in it, flag it for the human; never quietly fix, trim, or polish source text.
4. If the footage is an interview, a host and guest conversation taped for an interview or podcast, stop. That belongs to the interview production workflow, not this one.

## Lane 1: Written piece to video package

1. Get the source: the finished written piece (article, essay, review, newsletter issue), the target platforms, and any photos, footage, or artwork the writer has the rights to use.
2. Select the lines. Read the whole piece and choose the passages that will carry the video: usually the opening line, the strongest two or three beats, and the closing line. Present the selection as a numbered list, each line quoted exactly and tagged with where it comes from in the piece (paragraph or section), for the writer to approve or reorder before anything is built.
3. Order the package. Arrange the approved lines into the video’s running order. The script is the piece: the selected lines, in order, are the narration script or the on-screen text, with nothing written to connect them. If the running order needs a bridge the piece does not contain, flag the gap instead of writing one.
4. Build the shot and asset list. For each line or beat, name the visual: a photo, a clip, the artwork, or a text card. Use only assets the writer supplied or has the rights to. Flag every missing asset plainly; never substitute stock or generated imagery without the writer saying so.
5. Build captions from the same text. Captions and on-screen text use the selected lines word for word, cut into readable cues at phrase boundaries.
6. Make the cover from the piece’s own words: its headline or a selected line, set as the cover text. No new headline is written for the video.
7. Cut per platform from the same package: lengths, formats, and aspect ratios per destination (for example 9:16 vertical under 60 seconds for short-form feeds, 16:9 for a full-length upload). What changes per platform is format, length, and which lines lead. The wording never changes.
8. Ship it accessible: captions on every cut, alt text for the cover, and the full source text linked or attached so the video always points back to the piece.

## Lane 2: Long-form video to social clips (not interview tape)

For talks, panels, keynotes, and your own recorded videos and appearances, where there is no interviewer and interviewee.

1. Get the recording and its transcript (transcribe it first if none exists). Confirm the footage is not an interview; if it is, hand it to the interview production workflow.
2. Read the transcript for the moments that travel: the sharpest claim, the story, the line the audience reacted to. Propose each clip with its timestamp range, the exact opening words, and a search-friendly title written the way a viewer would search. A title may be punchy, but it may not promise something the speaker did not say.
3. Confirm the picks with the human before finalizing. They narrow and reorder the list.
4. Specify each clip’s captions, built from the recording’s own words, and its per-platform format (aspect, length, cover frame with a title drawn from what was actually said).
5. Deliver selects and specs only, the same discipline as interview production: the editor cuts, this skill makes the cut obvious and fast.

## Output contract

Deliver:

1. The selection list: every line or clip quoted or timestamped, with its source location, approved by the human.
2. The package spec per platform: running order, shot and asset list, captions text, cover text, format and length.
3. A gaps and flags list: missing assets, missing bridge lines, flagged wording, everything the human still owes. A gap is reported, never filled with invented material.
4. A short done and waiting-on note: what is ready to build or cut, and what is waiting on the human.

## Example

A 900-word album essay becomes a 60-second vertical video:

1. Selection: the essay’s opening line, one paragraph on the recording sessions (paragraph 4), the sentence about the closing track (paragraph 7), and the final line, each quoted exactly and tagged by paragraph.
2. Running order: opening line over the album artwork, the sessions paragraph as narration across three studio photos the writer supplied, the closing-track sentence as an on-screen text card, the final line over the cover.
3. One gap flagged: the writer supplied no photo for the sessions beat, so that slot waits on the writer instead of being filled.
4. Cuts: the 60-second 9:16 version leads with the opening line; a 16:9 version runs 2 minutes with one more paragraph restored. Same words, both versions.
